# Eight poems in Mesu

*Original compositions based on the Mesu reference, kenning lexicon, pronunciation guide, and the supplied sample poems.*

The IPA gives a broad reading of the written forms: pronounce every vowel fully and keep the syllables even. Established fossil words such as **was**, **lip**, and **mep** are transcribed as written. These fossils have final consonants other than **n**, although the pronunciation guide describes a stricter CV(n) pattern for ordinary syllables. Square brackets in the literal versions identify grammatical functions; they are not spoken.

## 1. One Star · plain observation

**Mesu**

> a wen nol na sos.  
> mi i mo na nul.  
> si i mo win mi.

**IPA**

> /a wen nol na sos/  
> /mi i mo na nul/  
> /si i mo win mi/

**Literal**

> [Exists] one small-light in up-stuff.  
> I [V] move in not-bright.  
> It [V] move with me.

**English**

> One star in the sky.  
> I walk in darkness.  
> It walks with me.

## 2. Two Waters · kenning and refrain

**Mesu**

> nuw i mo la nus.  
> was-lip i mo la nus.  
> nus i ku a pan was.

**IPA**

> /nuw i mo la nus/  
> /was.lip i mo la nus/  
> /nus i ku a pan was/

**Literal**

> Down-water [V] move toward down-stuff.  
> Water-eye [V] move toward down-stuff.  
> Down-stuff [V] receive [O] all water.

**English**

> Rain falls to the ground.  
> Tears fall to the ground.  
> The ground receives all water.

## 3. At Dusk · a message

**Mesu**

> lo-me-na e lo na nul we ne.  
> mi i se a wen me-to la tu.  
> mi i no te a tu.  
> lo-me-na e lo.

**IPA**

> /lo.me.na e lo na nul we ne/  
> /mi i se a wen me.to la tu/  
> /mi i no te a tu/  
> /lo.me.na e lo/

**Literal**

> Bright-say-place [QUAL] bright in dark-of-now.  
> I [V] send [O] one say-thing toward you.  
> I [V] not hear [O] you.  
> Bright-say-place [QUAL] bright.

**English**

> At dusk, the screen glows.  
> I send one word to you.  
> I cannot hear you.  
> The screen glows.

*Lo-me-na* is the screen expression in your own sample poem; *nul we ne* is the established “dark of now” dusk kenning.

## 4. Unspoken · courtly miniature

**Mesu**

> tu mep e ti.  
> mi mep e ti.  
> le-mep a wi-no-me.

**IPA**

> /tu mep e ti/  
> /mi mep e ti/  
> /le.mep a wi.no.me/

**Literal**

> Your say-part [QUAL] near.  
> My say-part [QUAL] near.  
> Two-mouth [IS-NOUN] feel-not-say.

**English**

> Your mouth is close.  
> My mouth is close.  
> A kiss is love without speech.

## 5. The Speech · a small satire

**Mesu**

> wo-mep i me a mu me-to.  
> nu-lum i se a was la tu.  
> tu i wan a was.

**IPA**

> /wo.mep i me a mu me.to/  
> /nu.lum i se a was la tu/  
> /tu i wan a was/

**Literal**

> Big-mouth [V] speak [O] many say-things.  
> New-person [V] give [O] water toward you.  
> You [V] drink [O] water.

**English**

> The big mouth speaks many words.  
> A child brings you water.  
> You drink the water.

*Wo-mep* is an established kenning that can mean either a leader or a windbag. The poem leaves that choice to the listener.

## 6. Near · distance and memory

**Mesu**

> tu pa i me a wen me-to.  
> mi i ki a ni me-to.  
> tu e nit.  
> ni me-to e ti.

**IPA**

> /tu pa i me a wen me.to/  
> /mi i ki a ni me.to/  
> /tu e nit/  
> /ni me.to e ti/

**Literal**

> You before [V] say [O] one say-thing.  
> I [V] have [O] this say-thing.  
> You [QUAL] far.  
> This say-thing [QUAL] near.

**English**

> You once said a word.  
> I have that word.  
> You are far away.  
> The word is near.

## 7. One Foot · a three-line turn

**Mesu**

> mi sop e nul.  
> mi mop i mo.  
> mi i mo.

**IPA**

> /mi sop e nul/  
> /mi mop i mo/  
> /mi i mo/

**Literal**

> My head [QUAL] dark.  
> My move-part [V] move.  
> I [V] move.

**English**

> My head is dark.  
> My foot moves.  
> I move.

## 8. Say-Stuff · a root-echo poem

**Mesu**

> mi i me a wen me-to.  
> ni me-to i mo la tu.  
> tu i me a ni me-to la mi.  
> mi kan tu a mesu.

**IPA**

> /mi i me a wen me.to/  
> /ni me.to i mo la tu/  
> /tu i me a ni me.to la mi/  
> /mi kan tu a me.su/

**Literal**

> I [V] say [O] one say-thing.  
> This say-thing [V] move toward you.  
> You [V] say [O] this say-thing toward me.  
> I and you [IS-NOUN] say-stuff.

**English**

> I say a single word.  
> It travels to you.  
> You speak it back to me.  
> You and I are language.

The repeated **me** moves from action to compound to the name **mesu**. The final line echoes the Mesu manifesto’s use of language as an identity, after a word has actually passed between two speakers.
